Daily Care

Diamonds are incredibly durable, but the settings and metals need gentle handling.

  • Put your diamond jewellery on after applying lotions, perfumes, and hairspray
  • Avoid wearing diamonds during heavy manual work or sports
  • Remove rings before washing dishes or using harsh cleaning products
  • Avoid contact with chlorine bleach — it can damage gold and platinum settings

Cleaning at Home

A simple cleaning routine keeps your diamond sparkling between professional inspections.

  • Soak in warm water with a few drops of mild dish soap for 15–20 minutes
  • Gently brush with a soft toothbrush, especially behind the diamond
  • Rinse under warm running water and pat dry with a lint-free cloth
  • Avoid ultrasonic cleaners unless recommended by your jeweller

Professional Maintenance

Even the strongest setting benefits from periodic professional attention.

  • Have your diamond inspected every 6–12 months
  • Professional cleaning removes buildup that home cleaning cannot
  • A jeweller can check prong tightness and setting security
  • Consider rhodium plating for white gold pieces to maintain brightness

Storage

Proper storage prevents scratches, chips, and loosened settings.

  • Store each piece separately — diamonds can scratch other jewellery
  • Use the original box, a soft pouch, or a lined jewellery compartment
  • Keep delicate pieces flat to avoid stress on clasps and prongs
  • Travel with a dedicated jewellery case to prevent tangling and damage

Insurance & Documentation

Protect your investment beyond just physical care.

  • Keep your certificate safe — it proves your diamond's quality
  • Consider jewellery insurance for high-value pieces
  • Photograph your diamond for identification purposes
  • Store certificates digitally as backups

Lab-Grown Diamond Care

Lab-grown diamonds require the same care as natural diamonds — they are chemically and physically identical.

  • Same cleaning and storage guidelines apply
  • Same durability — 10 on the Mohs hardness scale
  • Same resistance to heat, scratching, and chipping
  • The setting metal determines durability, not the diamond origin
